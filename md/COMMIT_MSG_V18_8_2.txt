V18.8.2: types/api.ts cleanup — drop ApiConfig.rateLimitMs (V18.8.1 already moved *Raw/*Response)

V18.8.2 — the type-system cleanup that follows the V18.8.1
apiClient cleanup. Two intended cleanups; one was already
done by the V18.1 type-contract revision.

What was already done (by commit 502a20c, the V18.1 type-
contract revision):

  - The `*Raw` and `*Response` interfaces were moved out of
    `src/types/api.ts` into the adapter files. The V18 type
    contract §2 requires `*Raw` DTOs to be **file-local** in
    each adapter; they never leak past the adapter boundary.
  - This was tracked as a V18.1 deliverable and is already
    done. There is nothing left to remove from `types/api.ts`
    in that category.

What was actually cleaned up in this commit:

  - `ApiConfig.rateLimitMs: number` — the field was already
    dead (the V18.8.1 commit removed the only consumer, the
    `rateLimit` helper in `apiClient.ts`), but the field was
    still on the type and the 10 per-service values were
    still in `constants/api.ts`. Now removed:
      - `src/types/api.ts`          : the field itself
      - `src/constants/api.ts`      : 10 `rateLimitMs: N,`
        lines across 9 service configs (tvmaze, musicbrainz,
        podcastIndex, radioBrowser, librivox, iptv, jamendo,
        internetArchive, audius, weather)

  - Doc comment on `ApiConfig.timeoutMs` updated to point
    at V18.8.1 (the reason rate limiting was removed).

Why rate limiting is gone for good (the design call):

  - TanStack's `useApiQuery` retry + dedup already prevents
    the obvious abuse (concurrent identical requests collapse
    to one in-flight call; failed requests retry with backoff).
  - The MusicBrainz "1 req/s" guidance was real and important
    for IP-API's "45 req/min/IP" but in practice the SIMBA
    app's traffic is well under any published limit. The
    weather adapter is the only one that ever published a
    "real" per-IP limit, and even that adapter doesn't call
    a public geo API anymore (it reads the device's location
    or the IANA timezone).
  - If a service later needs real backpressure, the right
    place is the adapter (a queue, a token bucket, or a
    throttled retry). The shared client shouldn't carry
    a per-service policy.
