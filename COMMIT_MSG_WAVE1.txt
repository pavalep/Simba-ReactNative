Close actually closes: teardown, lane, and the three missing controls

Four user-reported defects, one commit. All four are visible on the
home screen without opening anything.

1. "Closing doesn't actually close."

   VideoTitleOverlay's back called commands.exitPlayer(), which was
   only commands.exitPipAndFinish() - it finishes PlayerActivity and
   tears the SurfaceView down. The mpv engine is a process-global C++
   handle that outlives any Activity, so the window closed while the
   media carried on: audio still audible, notification still posted,
   engine still advancing, and no window left to stop it.

   exitPlayer() now calls commands.stopPlayback() FIRST, then
   dismisses. Order is the contract and is pinned by a test:
   finishing first tears the surface down while the engine still holds
   a decoded frame.

   The hardware back button had the same hole by a different route -
   there was no BackHandler at all, so RN popped the Activity and
   exitPlayer() never ran. Added, registered only while expanded so the
   background root does not swallow navigation back presses.

   Why video stops rather than continuing: audio has a mini bar
   always on screen to stop it again. Video has none - W6.0 removed the
   dock - so continuing means "playing with no way to stop it", or PiP,
   which is a separate chosen verb. Netflix, Plex and VLC all treat
   back-from-video as a stop for exactly this reason.

   The docblock that justified the old behaviour said minimize, back
   and close were "the ONE correct answer... the same action". It was
   wrong, and it is replaced with the reasoning rather than deleted.

2. A test that pinned the bug.

   useTransport.test.ts asserted the OPPOSITE - "stop+clear would kill
   playback that a minimise is supposed to preserve". The premise was
   false: there is no video minimise to preserve, and audio's minimise
   is a different verb that never calls exitPlayer. The suite spent
   its credibility defending the defect. Rewritten to pin the real
   contract.

3. The Turkish film with a music note.

   The seam discarded bridgeOpts.type, which is 'video' | 'audio' at
   every one of the ~31 launch sites, unless a screen happened to pass
   mediaKind/mediaLane - which 4 did. The checkpoint writer then
   forwarded no lane and normalizeMediaClassification filled the hole
   with 'audio'. Every badge consumer reads the lane, so a film drew a
   music note while Bookmarks showed it correctly.

   Fixed at the seam, not the card: the seam already had the truth and
   threw it away. A catalogue lane still wins when supplied.

   media.ts's backstop is now 'video' to match the bookmark writer.
   They previously defaulted the same omitted field to OPPOSITE values
   and both persisted it - one record, two answers, which is what made
   this unexplainable from the UI.

4. Volume, mute and queue in the audio player.

   HelpScreen.tsx:79 already told users to "tap the queue/list icon".
   No such control existed. It is not an invented affordance - Spotify,
   Apple Music, Plex, YouTube Music, VLC and Tencent Video all ship
   exactly this, and the app's own copy had been advertising it.

   Nothing needed building: TransportState already carried volume and
   isMuted, commands already had setVolume/setMuted, SvgIcon already
   had volume/volumeMute/listMusic, and the Queue route already existed.

   The queue minimizes before navigating - the player is painted over
   the navigator, so navigating without leaving it would stack a route
   behind an opaque view and the tap would look broken.

   Volume slider row is 44px tall; the video VolumeControl's 40px row
   is a landscape budget and does not survive portrait.

Also drops the duplicated "20-item cap" claim from the recent-history
docblock, which contradicted the 10 the code enforces.

Requires @simba-dev/react-native-media-player@1.13.1, which renames
stopAudioPlayback to stopPlayback - the body was never audio-specific
and the name became a lie the moment video needed it.
